Metric Glossary

Understanding the key metrics in basket analysis

Core Analytics Metrics

Total Transactions

The total number of customer orders/baskets in your dataset.

What it means: Higher numbers indicate more data for analysis.

Why it matters: With 100+ transactions, you get reliable patterns. Under 50 transactions may show inflated confidence levels.

Unique Products

The number of different products in your catalog.

What it means: How diverse your product range is.

Why it matters: More products allow for more association patterns. Very few products (< 10) may result in trivial rules.

Average Basket Size

The average number of items customers buy per transaction.

What it means: Typical spending pattern (items per order).

Why it matters: Higher basket size indicates better cross-selling. Size < 2 suggests customers buy single items.

Example: 3.5 items = customers typically buy 3-4 items per visit.

Average Reorder Rate

The percentage of products that customers repurchase.

What it means: Loyalty indicator - how often products are bought again.

Why it matters: High reorder rate (70%+) = customer loyalty. Low rate (< 30%) = one-time purchases.

Example: 65% reorder rate = 65% of products are repurchased.

Association Rule Metrics

Support

The percentage of transactions containing a product pair.

Formula: (# transactions with items A and B) / (total transactions) × 100%

What it means: How frequently an item set occurs together.

Why it matters:

  • Support > 10%: Very common pattern, high impact potential
  • Support 5-10%: Common pattern, good for recommendations
  • Support < 5%: Rare pattern, may be noise

Example: Support of 8% for (Bread, Butter) = appears in 8% of all transactions.

Confidence

The probability of buying product B given that customer already bought product A.

Formula: (# transactions with A and B) / (# transactions with A) × 100%

What it means: How strong the association is from A to B.

Why it matters:

  • Confidence > 70%: Very strong indicator, great for bundling
  • Confidence 40-70%: Moderate association, good for cross-selling
  • Confidence < 40%: Weak association, limited usefulness

Example: If someone buys Bread, 75% chance they also buy Butter.

Lift

How much more likely is product B to be purchased when product A is bought (compared to random chance).

Formula: Confidence / (% customers who buy B) = How many times more likely

What it means: The strength of the rule beyond what would be expected by chance.

Why it matters:

  • Lift > 2.0: Product B is 2x more likely! Excellent for bundling
  • Lift 1.5-2.0: Meaningful lift, good for recommendations
  • Lift 1.0-1.5: Slight positive correlation
  • Lift = 1.0: No relationship (independent)
  • Lift < 1.0: Negative correlation (customers avoid buying together)

Example: Lift of 3.0 for (Bread, Butter) means if someone buys Bread, they're 3x more likely to buy Butter than average.

Context Segmentation Metrics

Time of Day

Breaking down purchases by time segments.

Segments:

  • Morning: 5 AM - 12 PM
  • Afternoon: 12 PM - 5 PM
  • Evening: 5 PM - 10 PM

Why it matters: Different times have different shopping behaviors (breakfast items vs dinner items).

Budget Segment

Breaking down purchases by spending level (calculated from your data).

Calculation: Data-driven percentiles (33rd and 66th) based on your actual transaction values.

Segments:

  • Low: Bottom 33% of spenders
  • Medium: Middle 33% of spenders
  • High: Top 33% of spenders

Why it matters: Different customer segments buy different products. High-spenders may buy premium items.

Basket Size

Breaking down purchases by number of items in each transaction.

Segments:

  • Small: 1-3 items per basket
  • Medium: 4-8 items per basket
  • Large: 9+ items per basket

Why it matters: Bundle recommendations differ for quick-grab customers vs bulk buyers.

How to Use These Metrics

For Product Bundling:

Look for rules with Support > 8%, Confidence > 60%, and Lift > 2.0

For Cross-Selling:

Focus on high-confidence rules (40-70%) even if support is moderate

For Customer Loyalty:

Track reorder rate trends - increasing rate shows growing loyalty

Context-Based Recommendations:

Use time/budget/basket-size contexts to recommend specific products at the right moment

Important Note on Data Quality

The reliability of these metrics depends on your data quality:

  • Sample Size: At least 50-100 transactions recommended for reliable patterns (under 30 may show inflated metrics)
  • Time Range: Include diverse time periods and customer types
  • Product Variety: More diverse products = more discoverable patterns
  • Regular Updates: Refresh data periodically as shopping behaviors change seasonally
